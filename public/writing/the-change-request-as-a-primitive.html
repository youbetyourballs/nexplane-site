The Change Request as a Primitive

John Terrill · LinkedIn · /writing/the-change-request-as-a-primitive

In most organizations, the change request is paperwork. It describes the work someone intends to do, collects the signatures required by process, and then sits in a ticket system while the actual work happens somewhere else entirely. The ticket does not know what happened. The execution does not reference the ticket. The audit trail, such as it is, lives in two separate systems that have never been reconciled. That separation is the problem, and it is structural.

Nexplane treats the Change Request as the fundamental unit of infrastructure work. Not a form. Not a document. A primitive — a typed, structured object that carries the full state of a change from intent through outcome. Intent means what the operator is trying to accomplish and why. Scope means what systems and resources are in play. Dependencies means what else needs to be true before this change is safe to run. Those three fields alone make a change dramatically more legible than the average ticket.

But legibility is not enough. The CR also needs to be executable. The execution plan should live inside the same object as the intent. The approval should be recorded against the specific plan that was approved, not an abstract description of it. Verification signals — did the thing we changed actually behave correctly afterward — should be captured as part of the same record. Rollback should be a required field, not a comment in the notes section. When all of that lives in one object, the CR becomes the connective tissue between human judgment and machine execution rather than a document that gets filed and forgotten.

The audit trail that emerges from this approach is not a report someone runs at the end of the quarter. It is a continuous, structured record of every change that ever ran through the system, with full provenance. Who proposed it. What they proposed. Who approved it. What actually executed. What verification said. Whether rollback was ever needed. That record is operational intelligence. It surfaces patterns. It accelerates postmortems. It makes the organization smarter about the next change of the same type.

The change request as a primitive also changes how AI fits into infrastructure work. An AI that can reason about a well-structured CR — reading intent, checking dependencies, proposing a plan, flagging risk — is doing something genuinely useful. An AI that is handed an unstructured ticket and a shell prompt is improvising. The structure of the primitive is what makes AI assistance meaningful rather than merely fast.
