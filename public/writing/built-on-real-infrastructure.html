Built on Real Infrastructure

John Terrill · LinkedIn · /writing/built-on-real-infrastructure

There is a meaningful difference between software that demos well and software that survives contact with infrastructure. I have seen both. I have sat in vendor rooms watching polished slides, knowing that the moment you plug it into a real environment — with real blast radius, real legacy dependencies, real on-call rotations — things fall apart in ways the demo never anticipated.

Nexplane was not built as a slideware idea. It came from real operational frustration. Production systems that needed to change. Security risks that needed remediation. Teams that were reasonably afraid of making things worse. That fear was not irrational — it was well-earned from years of watching changes go sideways in ways that were entirely predictable in hindsight.

The hard part of infrastructure software is not generating a plan. The hard part is making a plan safe enough to trust. A plan that cannot be simulated, cannot be reviewed with meaningful context, and cannot be rolled back if it fails is not a plan — it is a hope. Nexplane is built around the premise that the execution model is the product, not the UI on top of it.

A good demo can hide complexity. A real system cannot. When I was building the earliest version of Nexplane, I ran every change against real infrastructure. Not mocks. Not simulated environments. Real systems, with real consequences, where a mistake would cost real time to clean up. That discipline shaped the architecture in ways that no amount of whiteboard design would have.

The first obligation of Nexplane is to real infrastructure. Not to the demo. Not to the slide. Real systems, real teams, real change — and a control plane that makes those changes safer, more understandable, and more reversible than doing them by hand.
