Your Change Management Process Is Probably Optimizing the Wrong Thing

John Terrill · LinkedIn · /writing/change-management-optimizing-wrong-thing

Ask why a change management process exists and you hear predictable answers: reduce outages, protect production, ensure compliance, maintain audit trails.

Those are reasonable. But they miss the deeper purpose.

A good change process should not primarily optimize approval. It should optimize understanding.

Most Change Processes Are Permission Systems

Someone fills out a form. Someone approves it. Maybe another team signs off. The change reaches production.

On paper, governance happened.

But did the organization understand the change better after all those approvals?

Often the answer is no.

Approval Is Not Understanding

A useful change request includes objective, assumptions, context, affected systems, rollback criteria, measurable success, confidence, and alternatives.

A weak request says: routine firewall update.

If both receive the same approval, the process is evaluating paperwork rather than engineering quality.

Better Governance

Every production change should leave the organization smarter.

Future engineers should be able to answer why the change was necessary, what alternatives existed, what assumptions mattered, and what happened afterward.

That is governance worth paying for.

The Five Outputs of Every Change

Every change should produce:

  1. Implementation
  2. Reasoning
  3. Prediction
  4. Observed outcome
  5. Lessons learned

Most organizations preserve only the first.

The other four are where engineering capability compounds.
