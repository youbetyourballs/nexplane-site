AI Shouldn't Have Root

John Terrill · LinkedIn · /writing/ai-shouldnt-have-root

The industry's biggest AI debate may be asking the wrong question.

Everyone seems focused on one problem:

> How do we safely give AI access to production?

That assumes the destination is correct.

Maybe it isn't.

Maybe AI shouldn't have production access at all.

Not directly.

Instead, maybe AI should have to go through the exact same structured change process we expect from human engineers.

Before any change reaches production, the system should answer questions like:

  • What exactly is changing?
  • Why is it changing?
  • What evidence supports the change?
  • What systems could be affected?
  • What assumptions are being made?
  • What does success look like?
  • How do we undo it if we're wrong?

Those aren't AI safety features.

They're simply good engineering.

The surprising thing is that most organizations don't consistently require humans to answer them either.

AI isn't creating a new engineering problem.

It's exposing an old one.

If we solve that problem well, the solution benefits both humans and AI.

The future probably isn't AI replacing engineers.

It's AI becoming another participant in a disciplined engineering change process where every consequential action is understandable, reviewable, observable, and reversible.

That's a much stronger safety model than simply trying to make an AI careful.

Because careful systems still make mistakes.

Well-designed systems make mistakes recoverable.

That difference matters.

Question: If you were designing AI infrastructure from scratch today, would you start by giving the AI production access—or by designing a better engineering change process?
